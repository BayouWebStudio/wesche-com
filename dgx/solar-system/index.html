Return only the HTML document, beginning with <!DOCTYPE html> and ending with </html>. Use only embedded HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Canvas 2D, WebGL, or inline SVG. Design a cinematic full-screen deep-space scene containing the Sun and all eight planets. Make each planet immediately recognizable through color, texture, rings, atmosphere, lighting, and scale while keeping the system visually usable rather than claiming literal astronomical scale. Include elliptical orbital paths, visibly different relative orbital speeds, Earth's Moon, several major moons around Jupiter, Saturn's rings, a dense asteroid belt, a layered starfield, and an occasional comet with a glowing tail. The solar system must continuously animate and feel spatial rather than like a flat diagram. Add smooth perspective depth, orbital inclination, planet rotation, glow, shadows, subtle bloom-like effects, and polished transitions while maintaining good frame rate. Required interactions: - drag or swipe to rotate the viewing angle; - mouse wheel or pinch-style controls to zoom; - click or keyboard-select any major body to smoothly focus the camera on it; - show a well-designed factual information panel for the selected body; - play/pause control; - simulation-speed control with a wide useful range; - toggles for labels, orbital paths, and motion trails; - an overview/reset control; - a clear way to leave focused-follow mode and return to the full system. Make the interface responsive on desktop and mobile, visually coherent, accessible by keyboard, and understandable without instructions. Use elegant typography, restrained glass-like panels, high contrast, and no visual clutter. Include a compact title and a small unobtrusive note that sizes and distances are artistically adjusted for visibility. Prioritize working interaction and animation over decorative complexity. Avoid placeholder elements, fake controls, static screenshots, broken features, and explanatory prose outside the HTML.
